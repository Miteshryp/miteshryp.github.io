# The Great Tragedy
It's an unfortunate tragedy that a dialouge from a show depicting the end of a world is aptly fitting the description of the social fabric of India. I'll take the liberty to elaborate on that in this post. I have consistently tried to restraint myself from any political posting in the past, as I've never seen the merit in adding to the noise, but this time I feel there is a line which has been crossed, and it's no longer okay to sit silent while this happens

If you're not living under a rock, you would know about the many scenes of protest which have surrounded Delhi for the past week, and any social media platform you would've tried to open seems to be flooded with reels either trying to extract meme and mockery content, or would be trying to paint the protestors as heroes of a social war waged against the degradation of democratic process. ## 1. A Look in the Past
As we all know, this is actually not the first protest of this nature. Mass mobilizations across social media, a protest agenda built around a rotten system and being told as a "systemic cover-up" by the opposition of India. Rings a bell? That's right - we recently had a CJP protest at Delhi which resulted in the removal of ex-education minister of India. At the first glance, this protest truely seemed to be the first of it's kind, and being in a GenZ world where all systems around us are constantly potrayed as failing and rotten - It almost made sense to take part in it. After all, who wouldn't want to support people who are proposing a revolution in our society, right?

Well if you pause right about now, and try to question this train of thought from the beginning - you'd see a bit of a pattern; that is if you're aware of how politics/protests and revolutions played out, the last time public was fed up of a rotten system. This [video](https://youtu.be/AZf9bOKjzv4?t=633) by Ganeshprasad from ThinkSchool describes an **"SAP framework"** used by new political parties to disrupt the grip and control of an existing political party and come to power. This framework was first used by Arvind Kejriwal in the 2012 elections to oust the long-sitting cheif minister of Delhi. Without digressing too much into the details of this, I'd like to draw some parallels from the current protest in comparison to the original protest against corruption which was led by Anna Hazare, but was eventually highjacked by AAP.

2012 was a time when the congress govt was at its lowest point in history. Revealation of one scam after another had left public in a state of disarray, and distrust towards the established poltical system was approaching its minima. This created a perfect atmostsphere for a competitor political player to take its place of power.
